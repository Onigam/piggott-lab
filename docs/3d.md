# 3D worlds

Five worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the only source: every constant says `reported`, `derived` or `estimate`; whatever
`src/sim/machine.ts` knows is imported, not retyped) with the frozen ids of `src/part-ids.ts`: `site` 8 parts, `turbine` 8,
`alternator` 10, `blades` 4, `shed` 6 (the technical room, added on 2026-10-29: the electronics and the battery had no view of their own). `src/three/index.ts` exports `createSiteScene()` (built at once) and `createShedScene()`, `createTurbineScene()`,
`createAlternatorScene()`, `createBladesScene()`, which return `lazyModule`s (nothing is built until the world is shown or read).
Checked by `*.test.ts` beside the code (83 tests): ids, floating and buried parts, explode, the kinematics of the tail, what turns
and what does not, the section caps, the labels of the default views, NaN-free animation over the whole range of the state.

## The machine (the video's, S3, with the plans' numbers, S4, where the wiki is silent)

Front (wind side) to rear: plywood triangle, the three blade roots (49 mm), 9 mm plywood disc, a washer and nut per rod, the hub plate,
the 230 × 6 mm steel disc with its resin ring and 8 magnets facing the stator, the 1 mm gap, the resin stator, the ⌀ 140 × 8 mm cradle,
the 206 mm angle iron with the yaw pipe under it. The stub axle ("fusée", solid, ⌀ 25.4) is screwed on the cradle through the housing
flange and goes forward through the stator and the rotor to the nut in the hub plate; the barrel of the hub (⌀ 60, two bearings) turns on
it. The stator is cast on **three M12 × 70 rods** (ψ = 0°, 90°, 270°: the fourth hole of the cradle is lost under the weld) nutted on the
cradle, which set the gap. The rotor disc is bolted to the hub plate by **four M12 × 140 rods with 16 nuts** (4 per rod: one cast in the
rotor, one on the plate, two on the front of the blade assembly). The tail hinge is pipe over pipe: a 33.4 × 150 mm pipe welded to the
end of the angle iron (20° from the vertical, 55° in plan view), a 42.2 × 130 mm sleeve with a cap plate that swings on it, the 500 mm
boom welded to the sleeve, a 12 in T-bar across the boom's end and the 18 × 12 in (0.135 m²) 6 mm plywood vane bolted to it.

**What turns with `rotorAngle`**: the bearing hub with its four rods, the magnet rotor, the magnets, the blades and the plywood. **What
never turns**: stub axle with its cradle, stator, coils, star wiring, the three stator rods, the castle nut (it is in the `bearing-hub`
part, in a fixed sub-group), the whole frame.

## Frames

Alternator frame: X along the axle, front towards -X, origin in the mid-plane of the stator, the section plane is z = 0, ψ from +Y
towards +Z. Head frame: the alternator frame moved so that the origin is on the yaw axis (the axle at z = +41 mm); the wind blows towards
+X; the head turns about +Y by `yaw` (positive = tail towards -Z in the head frame, the rotor turns to face +Z), the tail by `tailSwing`
about the hinge axis through the sleeve (a turn of -φ about the axis takes the boom towards +Z; it rises by 27° at 95°).

While anything is exploded, hidden or a wire frame, the head is held in its rest pose (yaw 0, tail on its stop).

## Explode: the insertion paths (the guide plays the offsets backwards)

- **alternator**: along the axle, to the front, in the order things come off: hub with its rods (-0.30 m; its barrel slides out of the
  rotor and the stator), magnet rotor (-0.20), magnets (-0.15, out of the pockets towards the stator), coils (-0.10, out of their
  pockets), star wiring (-0.10), stator with its cast-in rods (-0.04); the stub axle with its cradle goes back (+0.10). The gauge
  comes out of the gap upwards (+0.16 m), the moulds slide aside (+0.30 m).
- **turbine**: rotor forward (-0.32) off the rods, alternator forward (-0.12) off the angle iron, the frame with the hinge lifts 0.20 m off
  the stub, the mast piece goes down (-0.15), the tail sleeve slides 0.25 m up along the hinge axis relative to the hinge, the vane slides
  0.25 m sideways off the T-bar, the cable is drawn aside (+0.30 m in Z).
- **blades**: the plywood disc lifts 0.10 m off the post, the blades 0.30 m, the triangle 0.55 m (a `custom` of the registry), blank and
  templates 0.30 m. **site**: every part rises by its own amount (`EXPLODE` in `site/scene.ts`), the technical room as one part. **shed**: the five devices come off the board towards the viewer, alternately 0.6 and 0.95 m, and the battery up 0.6 m and forward 0.8 m (`EXPLODE` in `shed/scene.ts`).

- `xrayShell`: site `['obstacles', 'house']`; turbine `['rotor', 'nacelle-frame', 'yaw-bearing']`; alternator `['magnet-rotor', 'bearing-hub', 'stator']`;
  blades `['hub-disc']`; shed none (no part of the room shelters another).

  `{ padding: 1.6, minRadius: 0.05 }` / 2.0; blades `{ padding: 1.5, minRadius: 0.08 }` / 2.2; shed `{ padding: 1.6, minRadius: 0.25 }` / 2.4.

  searched with the replica of the kit's framing (`framing.ts`) so that every part of the overview lies above the floating toolbar (about the bottom 100 px of
  the 842 × 678 view); the kit keeps the room itself on a narrow view (bottom inset, tight fit of the extents).
- Labels: `labels.test.ts` replicates the overview camera and the label box at 842×678, 842×736, 380×902 and 390×632 (FR and EN) and requires

`merge.ts` bakes the static meshes of a part into one mesh per material (colours in the vertices); what moves (lever, LEDs, glowing element,
charge segments, window panes, the three spinning groups) is merged inside its own group or flagged `dynamic`. `shared.test.ts` builds the alternator with
and without merging and compares every part. No geometry or material is created per frame (the mast and the guys are rebuilt only when the height or the diameter changes).

## Decisions and simplifications (what the sources do not say, flagged `estimate` in `dims.ts`)

- **Settled against `docs/data.md` "Disagreements"**: one magnet rotor (S3 steps 17-26, S4 p. 44); hub plate = ply 9 mm disc of 200 mm (the plans' disc; 140 × 8 mm is the
  steel cradle) + a 9 mm triangle on the other side (S3 step 6); yaw pipe 100 mm (S3 step 24); vane 6 mm; stator rods 3 × M12 × 70 (S3 step 9); ONE 100 Ah battery
  (the simulation default). Boom 500 mm and vane 18 × 12 in follow the plans (S3: 700 mm, about 0.1 m²). The plans put the rectifier at the battery (drawn in the shed).
- **Layout the sources leave open** (estimates): the hub plate is in front of the rotor and its barrel goes back through the rotor and the stator (S3 step 18: the bell is cut
  down to a flat plate; the 60 mm recess of the plywood disc, step 7, clears the end of the stub axle); the yaw axis stands under the middle of the angle iron; the cable
  leaves the stator by a hole of the cradle (ψ = 300°), runs on the angle iron and goes down through the 14 mm hole; the mast piece and the bench block are workshop props.
- **Diagrammatic**: the star wiring (the real links are cast in the resin): half-hoops on the rim edge for the series links, a ring for the neutral, three leads to the cradle hole.
  The notch of the tail sleeve is replaced by a tab that rests on a pin of the hinge pipe. Resin is drawn opaque amber, the coil cores flush with the faces.
- **Blades**: the leading edge is the straight edge of the plank (the taper is on the trailing side), stations at 100·i mm, thickness measured straight up from the flat face, the wing
  section a sine profile peaking at 30 % of the chord; root = the 49 mm block with the 120° wedge (60° cuts).
- **Site**: the shed has no roof and no left wall; its back wall, its right wall and the board fade (opacity 0.06) when the camera is on their outer side, whatever the direction the guide or the visitor looks from, so the devices are never behind a wall; the cable run is L-shaped (about 15 m with the wiring); thin lines (mast, guys,
  cable) are widened to 2.2 px from afar by a shader and picked by distance to the centre line; the guys are 4 mm drawn true; the mast grows with the rotor (radius × diameter / 1.2).
  The trees stand west and north-west (upwind), 40 of them, 0.7-1.1 × `obstacleHeight`; none when it is 0.
- **Not checked in a browser** (the lead does): the look of the merged colours, the shader widening, the section against the caps, the sky at night, the label dots on the real stage.

## The technical room (`shed`)

`src/three/shed/room.ts` builds the shed once (`buildShed()`: slab, back and right walls that fade when the camera is behind them, the
plywood board, the six devices, the wire of each one, and `animateShed()` for the lever, the lamps, the glowing element and the charge
bar); the site draws it as scenery (one registered part, `technical-room`) and `src/three/shed/scene.ts` makes it a world of its own at the
size of the room (a garden shed, estimate): the brake switch, the rectifier, the fuse, the controller and the dump load on the board in
the order of the current, the battery on the floor in front, the mast's cable coming in from a post on the open left side and the
supply to the house leaving on the right (scenery). "Flows" runs the dots along the cable, through the devices to the battery, in the
dump load and out to the house, as in the site. The six labels sit on three levels so that the names never overlap, the two on the
right pulled a little to the left so that they stay inside the picture on a phone. No section: the room has no part that is hollow in a
way worth cutting yet (a cut battery with its plates and cells would be the natural next step).
